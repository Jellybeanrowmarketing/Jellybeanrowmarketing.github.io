Google Ads for St. John's Roofers and Plumbers

Tyler York  ·  Jellybean Row Marketing  ·  November 13, 2026

A homeowner in St. John's with water in the ceiling, a pipe that let go, or siding that came loose looks for someone who can come to the house. Searches such as “roofer St. John's,” “emergency plumber,” “siding repair Mount Pearl,” and “heat pump installer Paradise” are already about a job. A Google Search ad can show on that search. You choose the towns, the hours, and the amount. The click has to reach a person who can take the work.

Match the ad to one job

A Search campaign shows ads to people who are actively searching for a product or service. Keywords are the words you want that search to line up with. Google's guide to creating a Search campaign says each ad group should stay relevant to what people are searching for. Roof repair, an emergency plumbing call, a heat pump install, and a siding quote each need their own ad and their own page.

Match types set how close the search has to be. Broad match is the default. The ad may show on related searches, including ones that do not carry the keyword's meaning. Phrase match covers searches that include the meaning of the keyword. Exact match covers the same meaning or the same intent, and it reaches fewer searches. Google's keyword matching options sets out the three. When you create the campaign, leave Google search partners and the Display Network off if you only want ads on Google search.

Leave out searches that are not a customer

A search for roofer jobs in St. John's, a plumbing course, a how-to, or materials you do not sell can sit next to the searches you want. Negative keywords keep the ad off the terms you list. They do not cover close variants, so excluding “job” still allows “jobs.” Google's negative keywords page says to add those versions yourself, and that too many exclusions can mean fewer people see the ad. Check the searches that triggered the ad.

Only the towns the truck can reach

You can target cities, or a radius around an address of at least 1 kilometre. A very small target may show only some of the time, or not at all. When you type a city, add Canada, so you get St. John's in Newfoundland. Google's location targeting guide includes that step.

Presence or Interest is the default. It includes people likely to be in the places you chose, or regularly in them, and people who have shown interest in those places. Presence keeps the ads for people likely to be located, or regularly located, there. Google's advanced location options describe this as a best effort, and a perfect match is not guaranteed. Use Presence, and only the towns the truck can reach. Most campaigns see fewer impressions when they leave that default. A Paradise crew that already works St. John's, Mount Pearl, and Conception Bay South can include those. You can also exclude one place inside a wider area.

Answer the phone the ad offers

A call asset adds your number to the ad. On a phone, a call button can show under the ad. On a computer, a Call us button can show a number for the business. A click on that button costs the same as a click on the headline, and the button does not show on every impression. Typing the number into the ad text may get the ad disapproved. Google checks that the number belongs to the business. Google's call assets page covers this, and you can limit that button to hours someone will answer.

An ad schedule can limit the whole campaign to certain days and hours. Until you set one, ads are eligible all day. If nobody answers on Sunday, leave Sunday off. A scheduled campaign still paces toward a monthly spend of 30.4 times the average daily budget, whatever portion of the month the ads are eligible. Google's ad scheduling page states that rule. Pause the campaign when the crew cannot take another job.

Open the click on the right page

The final URL is the page the click opens. Use the page for that job. On a phone it should name the work, the towns you serve, and a number someone answers. Name the work you do, such as asphalt shingles, heat pump service, or siding repair. Keep the hours on the page the same as the schedule. The Google listing is covered in our local SEO note.

You set the budget. A daily budget is an average. Google may spend more on a busy day, and the month averages out to the amount you entered. A campaign total budget does not go past the amount you set, between a start date and an end date. Both are in the Search campaign guide. Clicks are paid to Google. Management is a separate fee, on our paid ads page.
